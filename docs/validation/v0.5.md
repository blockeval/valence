## Four-level p99 dose experiment

The paired design held p50 at 150 ms and p95 at 800 ms. Target p99 was:

| Condition | Target p99 |
|---|---:|
| Low | 1,500 ms |
| Moderate | 3,000 ms |
| High | 6,000 ms |
| Extreme | 12,000 ms |

All four conditions used the same 20 seeds. The topology was a degree-two
connected overlay with fanout two. This setting avoids both the severe ceiling
from fanout one and the complete tail masking seen in dense redundant overlays.

### Paired comparisons with the low-p99 condition

After Holm correction across the 12 primary comparisons:

- moderate p99 increased stale-head attestations by 0.0516
  (95% bootstrap CI 0.0445 to 0.0584; adjusted p = 0.000240);
- high p99 reduced mean slot-level stake-weighted head agreement by 0.00845
  (CI -0.01253 to -0.00487; adjusted p = 0.000854);
- high p99 increased stale-head attestations by 0.1070
  (CI 0.0912 to 0.1236; adjusted p = 0.000240);
- high p99 increased the below-0.9 divergence rate by 0.0290
  (CI 0.0177 to 0.0403; adjusted p = 0.001465);
- extreme p99 reduced head agreement by 0.0266
  (CI -0.0343 to -0.0193; adjusted p = 0.000240);
- extreme p99 increased stale-head attestations by 0.1379
  (CI 0.1156 to 0.1611; adjusted p = 0.000240); and
- extreme p99 increased the below-0.9 divergence rate by 0.0887
  (CI 0.0661 to 0.1113; adjusted p = 0.000240).

Finality lag did not change. This is a useful null result: the tested tail range
caused increasing transient disagreement and stale voting without crossing the
simplified finality boundary.

### Dose-response tests

Per doubling of target p99:

- stake-weighted head agreement decreased by 0.00881 on average;
- stale-head attestation rate increased by 0.04691;
- below-0.9 slot divergence increased by 0.02952; and
- finality lag remained unchanged.

The first three dose-response tests remained significant after Holm correction
(adjusted p = 0.000080 each). These figures are engineering validation outputs,
not final paper estimates; final results should be rerun from a tagged release
and reported with the generated per-seed tables.

## Distribution-family diagnostic

A five-seed diagnostic held p50 and p95 calibration targets at approximately
150 ms and 800 ms for the analytic families. It confirmed distinct p99 and
p99.9 behavior for Weibull, lognormal, log-logistic, quantile-piecewise, and a
lognormal/generalized-Pareto splice. A Markov-modulated model was also run to
validate temporal-state reporting.

The distribution comparison is diagnostic because not all families can match
three arbitrary quantiles simultaneously. Its purpose is to verify that VALENCE
reports the resulting calibration error and protocol outcomes rather than
silently claiming exact matching.

Reference artifacts are under `validation/reference/`.
